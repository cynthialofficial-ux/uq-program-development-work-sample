What evidence would justify moving a program idea into a full business case?

This work sample shows how I would structure the analysis behind a program-development decision: define the question, identify the evidence that is still missing, specify the data pull, test demand and load, and bring the result back as a clear recommendation.

Scope. The example uses an illustrative short-form postgraduate concept in Digital Service Transformation. It is not a UQ proposal. Public UQ information is treated as fact; internal data requirements are stated explicitly; numerical demand and cost assumptions are synthetic.
Open the working workbook See the data specification
01 · Working question

Before modelling a positive case, test whether the proposal deserves one.

UQ's public program-approval procedure requires new and major initiatives to address rationale, consultation, resources and potential duplication. PBI's role adds the demand, load, pricing and viability evidence needed to make that decision useful.

Working view: further evidence required.
The public information is enough to identify the questions, but not enough to support a launch recommendation. I would first test portfolio overlap, actual demand, intake/load and the funding/price case.
02 · Evidence register

Separate what is known, what needs internal data, and what would change the decision.

Rather than score a concept with an arbitrary framework, I would keep a live evidence register. Each question has a source, a quality check and a next action.

03 · Comparator review

Use public programs as evidence examples, not as a complete market-size answer.

The workbook records comparable UQ offerings and a small number of external programs. The point is to test cohort, outcome, delivery mode and price on a like-for-like basis, then replace the public scan with UQ internal performance data before a business-case recommendation.

Internal portfolio

Graduate Certificate in Business Analytics, Master of Business Analytics and Master of Business create a real overlap question. The next step is not another web search; it is to pull their applications, commencements, EFTSL, retention and learner profile.

External market

UNSW and Deakin show that 'digital transformation' is already a visible postgraduate proposition. That makes differentiation, learner segment and delivery format more important than the label itself.

05 · Demand, load and viability

Keep the model small enough to challenge.

1

Applications

Start from comparable application history rather than a top-down 'market size'.

2

Conversion

Use offer and acceptance rates to estimate commencements.

3

Load

Translate commencements and retention into new and returning EFTSL.

4

Viability

Test funding, price and incremental cost under transparent sensitivities.

The workbook contains synthetic formulas only to demonstrate the mechanics. A real case would use UQ Reportal / Load Model data and agreed Finance / fee assumptions.
06 · Working plan

Move from sponsor question to decision brief in a controlled sequence.

The analysis would move in stages, with each stage producing a defined output and an evidence threshold for the next decision. The aim is to keep the work proportionate: resolve the highest-value uncertainty before building a larger business case.

FrameConfirm problem, target cohort, sponsor and decision required.
Internal portfolioPull comparable UQ performance and test duplication / cannibalisation.
External marketValidate learner need, competitor positioning and price / format.
Demand & loadBuild applications-to-commencements and new/returning EFTSL scenarios.
Viability & feasibilityTest funding, incremental cost, capacity, policy and launch constraints.
RecommendPrepare a short decision note: proceed, validate, reshape or stop.
